Branch Compatibilism

A version of compatibilism that takes the Everett many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics as its physical footing. It is a philosophical position that uses a physical theory. It is not a new interpretation of quantum mechanics.

Classical compatibilism says free will and determinism can both be true, but it does not say why the feeling of choice persists. Branch Compatibilism offers a candidate mechanism: free will is which branch of a fully deterministic wavefunction a conscious thread comes to experience.

Premises

P1. One wavefunction. The universe is a single wavefunction that evolves deterministically, with no collapse. Worlds are an emergent description of decoherent branches within it.

P2. Selection, not causation. Consciousness does not change how the wavefunction evolves. It is the capacity to experience one determinate path through it instead of the whole superposition.

P3. The thread. You are neither a moment nor a persistent substance. You are a thread, a continuous sequence of cross-sections through configuration space.

Free will as branch selection

Since P1 rules out changing the wavefunction, free will cannot be that. It is the thread being directed toward one branch instead of another. Determinism holds completely at the level of the wavefunction. What is not settled for the thread is which branch it occupies next.

Classical compatibilism defines freedom as acting on your own unimpeded desires, without saying what makes them your own. Here the thread is the referent.

A gradient of selection

Selection is not only for discrete decisions. It runs across a range.

  • Near term. Explicit decisions, such as going to work or not.
  • Diffuse. Dispositions, beliefs and self-concept, which bias selection across many branches over time.
  • Long range. Karma, read as accumulated disposition. It is a shape to the thread's tendencies, not a causal ledger.

If prayer or manifestation do anything, they would act here, as orientation that shifts selection bias, and not as a cause acting on the wavefunction. This is almost certainly unfalsifiable, and it sits in tension with the empirical work on intercessory prayer.

Why choice feels the way it does

Compatibilists usually treat the sense that you could have done otherwise as a confusion to be dissolved. Here it tracks something real. Every outcome exists in the wavefunction regardless, so you have no freedom over outcomes. You do have freedom over which sequence of outcomes is your history. The feeling of choice is an accurate readout of the one thing that is yours.

Responsibility and identity

All branches occur regardless of selection, so the position says little about responsibility to others. It supports responsibility to yourself: cultivating the orientation that selects toward the branches, and the future self, you would want. This loosely matches the Buddhist idea of cetanā, intention shaping future experience, but with a literal structural referent.

Identity is thread continuity. That gives Parfit's reductionist view a physical basis: degrees of continuity are literal branching structure.

Consciousness as a shape

A speculative model: consciousness is a shape that intersects configuration space, and the intersection at any instant is the present moment, like a hypersphere passing through a plane. That gives the present a referent, the thread's current cross-section. It also raises a tension. If the thread is a complete, fixed shape, is anything left to select, or is selecting just how a cross-section feels from inside a fixed trajectory? This is left open.

Other minds

If only one consciousness-shape is active, other people's inner lives might be a byproduct of your selection. This does not deny that they physically exist, since every version of every person is in the wavefunction. It denies only that meeting them requires their shape to be switched on.

The preferred resolution is symmetry. Every thread is in this position at once, each the active one from its own vantage. No thread is privileged, which avoids classical solipsism's unjustified asymmetry.

What it does not claim

  • It does not solve the hard problem of consciousness. It assumes consciousness can have a thread.
  • It is not currently falsifiable. Its value is interpretive: an account of why the tension between determinism and free will feels so stubborn.
  • It makes no claims about ethics toward others.

Open questions

The memory problem

Memory is physical configuration, identical whichever thread produced it. Two branches that converge on the same brain state have the same memory traces, and nothing marks which thread they belong to. Two directions are open. Memory could be passive content, with continuity simply lived. Or memory could be an active constraint on which states can follow which, though that risks circularity.

Position and momentum of the thread

Perhaps a cross-section encodes not only a configuration but also a direction, where the thread is headed. Two branches with identical configurations could differ in trajectory. This may resolve the memory problem without circularity, and it may leave selection real at the level of momentum even if position sequences are fixed. This is the most promising line for further work.

Related work

The closest existing view is the many-minds interpretation of Albert and Loewer. Open individualism is related but inverted: it holds that there is really one shared consciousness, while here each thread is locally the one. Wallace's The Emergent Multiverse covers how probability works for branching observers, which likely bears on the gradient model.
